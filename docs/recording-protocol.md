# Recording protocol — draft

1. Use a fixed phone support and bright, steady lighting. Start with a slowly oscillating flexible target; do not modify fan blades or remove guards.
2. Place a high-contrast marker on the moving structure and include a textured stationary reference nearby. Keep both regions separate and in view.
3. Document phone, capture mode, verified acquisition FPS, frame timing, resolution, exposure if available, distance and target. Disable stabilization if the device supports it; document the setting.
4. Record at least 10 seconds per condition, with three repeats. Include a static baseline and a deliberately poor capture. Keep geometry consistent for comparisons.
5. At 30 fps, begin with approximately 2–5 Hz controlled motion. This is a test choice, not a validated operating range or protection against higher-frequency aliasing.
6. Analyze both projected axes. Preserve raw videos, regions, parameters and exported CSVs. Report failed tracking and repeat variation alongside successes.
7. Once available, mount the MPU6050 on the same moving structure. Log device timestamps and acceleration units, sampling configuration, mounting and orientation. Sensor mass can change motion. Verify sampling regularity before spectral comparison.

No physical results yet. Do not describe synthetic test outcomes as industrial validation.
